# Dimensions

Six neutral dimensions, not tied to any company ladder. If an org framework turns up later these
remap onto it, because the evidence records what happened rather than a rating.

| Dimension | What it covers |
|---|---|
| `impact` | The outcome changed for users or the business, not the activity performed |
| `scope` | Size of the problem owned end to end, and how far beyond their own work it reaches |
| `technical depth` | Judgement in hard calls, and finding causes others missed |
| `ownership` | Responsibility taken unprompted, especially for unglamorous or ambiguous work |
| `collaboration` | Making others more effective; behaviour under disagreement |
| `communication` | Clarity, and telling people things before they have to ask |

## What counts as evidence

Three things, and it fails without any one:

1. **A date** — when it happened, not when you wrote it down.
2. **An incident** — specific, in a sentence someone else could verify.
3. **A source** — your observation, a named artefact (`INI-005`, a review thread), or a named
   person's feedback.

Adjectives are conclusions, not evidence. "Great communicator", "needs to grow scope" cannot be
defended in calibration, or fairly to the person. Ask for the incident behind the adjective — if
there isn't one, that itself is worth knowing: the impression came from somewhere other than the
work.

## What is not evidence

- **Delivery outcomes alone.** An initiative slipping is a fact about the initiative. It becomes
  evidence about a person only with the manager's specific account of what they did.
- **Absence.** Not having done something is evidence about opportunity, which is the manager's
  problem to fix.
- **Comparisons.** "Better than X at this" describes X.
- **Second-hand sentiment** with no named source. "People find them difficult" is a rumour until
  someone owns it.

## Coverage

At review time report per dimension: how many rows, over what span, from how many distinct
sources. Say plainly which dimensions are unevidenced. Two patterns to flag:

- **Recency clustering** — everything from the last six weeks, so the review describes a month
  rather than a year. The fix is a habit, not more writing.
- **Single-source** — every row from the manager's own observation. Fragile in calibration, and
  it may mean the person's work is invisible outside the team, which is itself a finding.
